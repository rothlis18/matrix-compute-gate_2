**South Korea's Low Fertility Rate: A Quantitative and Sociological Analysis**

**1. Contributing Factors:**
   - **High Housing Costs:** The exorbitant cost of housing in South Korea makes it difficult for young adults to afford homes or apartments, delaying marriage and childbearing.
   - **Educational Pressures:** The intense focus on academic achievement creates high stress among students and parents, deterring childbearing due to perceived financial and social burdens.
   - **Career-Oriented Lifestyles:** Long working hours and professional demands leave little time for family life, with significant pressure to succeed professionally.

**2. Structural Mechanisms:**
   - These factors create a system where child-rearing is seen as a significant financial and social burden, discouraging early marriages and larger families.

**3. Dependency Ratio and Impact on Systems:**
   - A declining fertility rate increases the elderly population, shifting the dependency ratio. A 25% decrease in the working-age population could necessitate a 50% tax increase or a 30% reduction in public services, straining pension and healthcare systems.

**4. Policy Interventions:**
   - Financial support for families, improved parental leave policies, and affordable housing initiatives are crucial. Structural changes, such as education system reforms and promoting work-life balance, are necessary to address root causes.

**5. Long-Term Implications:**
   - A declining population could lead to economic stagnation, affecting innovation and national power. Comprehensive policies addressing economic, social, and cultural factors are essential to mitigate these effects.

In conclusion, South Korea's low fertility rate is a complex issue driven by economic and social factors, necessitating multifaceted policy interventions to sustain economic and social stability.
