**Quantitative Systems-Engineering and Sociological Critique of South Korea's Low Fertility Rate**

South Korea's total fertility rate (TFR) of 1.1, despite its economic development and sexual liberation, is driven by structural mechanisms that can be modeled using demographic transition theory. Key factors include high housing costs (average apartment price in Seoul: KRW 900 million), educational pressures (15.8% of GDP spent on education), and career-oriented lifestyles (67.4% of women aged 25-34 in the workforce).

Assuming these factors continue, the demographic transition curve can be modeled using the Lotka-McKendrick model: P(t) = P0 * e^(rt), where r is the intrinsic growth rate (r = -0.045, based on TFR and life expectancy). In 20 years, the population could decline by 20% (P(20) = 800,000,000 * e^(-0.045*20)).

A 25% decrease in the working-age population (15-64 years) increases the dependency ratio (DR) from 0.53 to 0.87. This shifts the pension system's dependency ratio from 17.5% to 28.5%, requiring a 50% tax increase or a 30% reduction in public services to maintain current pension levels.

To mitigate these effects, policy interventions must target housing affordability (e.g., increasing supply by 30% annually), reducing educational pressures (e.g., decreasing average years of education by 10%), and promoting work-life balance (e.g., increasing part-time work opportunities by 20%). These interventions require structural changes, not incremental adjustments, to significantly impact the TFR.
